Scientific Methodology & Experimental Design

To evaluate detection efficacy in the absence of real-world labeled fraud datasets (which are legally confidential), we simulated a realistic psychometric examinee cohort of {{ eval_res.total_examinees }} candidates across 30 examination centers taking a 60-item test. Examinees were generated with latent ability parameters ($\theta \sim \mathcal{N}(0.55, 0.16)$), item difficulty coefficients, and popular distractor frequencies. Two specific malpractice typologies were injected: individual peer-to-peer collusion (copying) and organized center-wide question paper leaks (affecting {{ eval_res.injected_cheaters }} candidates).
